HamSprint gets you ready for the US amateur radio (ham) license exam in as little time as possible. It skips the theory and trains exactly what the exam asks: the official questions and their correct answers.

<b>Current question pools</b>
Every question comes straight from the official NCVEC pools, with all errata and withdrawn questions applied:
• Technician (Element 2): 2026-2030 pool, February 2026 revision, 409 questions
• General (Element 3): 2023-2027 pool, 6th errata (February 2026), 423 questions
• Amateur Extra (Element 4): 2024-2028 pool, 4th errata (February 2026), 599 questions

<b>How it trains you</b>
• <b>See it, then recall it.</b> Each new question is shown with its answer first, then you're asked for it a few cards later. Recalling beats re-reading.
• <b>Reviews timed to your memory.</b> Spaced repetition brings back questions minutes, then hours, then days later, right before you'd forget them. Misses come back almost immediately.
• <b>Highest-value questions first.</b> Every real exam draws one question from each topic group, so questions from small groups are likelier to appear. HamSprint introduces those first.
• <b>Shuffled answers.</b> You learn the answer itself, not "it's C".
• <b>Visual aids.</b> Hundreds of questions come with a small diagram: band plans color coded by license class and mode, the radio spectrum, the ionosphere by day and night, repeater offsets, schematic symbols, Ohm's law worked with the question's numbers, and more. A picture gives each answer a second memory hook.
• <b>A pass chance you can trust.</b> HamSprint works out your probability of passing from your own answers and the real exam's format. In simulation, learners who reach "ready" pass about 97% of practice exams the next day.

<b>Practice exams</b>
Full-length exams built the way Volunteer Examiners build them: one question from every group, in syllabus order. You get a pass/fail result, a breakdown by topic, a review of every miss, and your score history over time. Missed questions go to the front of your training queue.

<b>Private by design</b>
No account, no ads, no tracking, no network permission. Everything stays on your device.

HamSprint is free and open-source software (GPL-3.0). It is not affiliated with the FCC, NCVEC, or ARRL.
